LECTURE 01

CORE PRINCIPLES

Intro &
Security Goals

Security is not a single feature. It is a balance of protections that lets people and systems work with confidence.

01

The CIA triad

The three foundational objectives of information security guide how we design, assess, and protect every system.

C

Confidentiality

Information is accessible only to authorized people and systems.

Encryption · Access control
I

Integrity

Information remains accurate, complete, and protected from improper change.

Hashes · Digital signatures
A

Availability

Authorized users can access information and services when needed.

Backups · Redundancy
02

Extended security concepts

Accountability

Actions can be traced to the responsible party.

Authenticity

Users, systems, and data are genuinely what they claim to be.

Assurance

Confidence that controls work as intended and meet their requirements.

Anonymity

A subject can act without revealing or being linked to an identity.

LECTURE 01

THREAT LANDSCAPE

Attacks &
Adversaries

Understanding who may act against a system, and how visible their actions are, is central to threat modeling.

01

Network attacks

PASSIVE

Observe without changing

An attacker monitors communication to learn from it. Passive attacks are difficult to detect because no data is altered.

Examples: eavesdropping, traffic analysis

ACTIVE

Alter or disrupt

An attacker modifies messages, injects data, impersonates a party, or prevents normal service.

Examples: replay, masquerade, denial of service
02

Threat actors

Insiders

People with legitimate access who misuse it deliberately or accidentally.

Crackers / Hackers

Technically capable actors who seek to exploit weaknesses for a range of motives.

Script-Kiddies

Inexperienced attackers who rely on tools or scripts made by others.

Spies & Criminals

Organized actors pursuing intelligence, financial gain, or strategic advantage.
